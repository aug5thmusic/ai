SOURCE CONVENTIONS
The music is transcribed in concert pitch with the source clefs and keys.
Written measure numbers restart at 1 for the Trio. Both alternate endings
count in the written numbering: Trio 12/13 and 31/32. Pitch names below use
scientific pitch notation, with C4 = middle C.

Repeats, slurs, staccato dots, dynamics, hairpins, triplets, trills, grace
notes, and single-stroke measured tremolos are retained. Unslashed small
quarter notes are set as appoggiaturas; slashed small eighth notes are set
as acciaccaturas. Old-style quarter rests use modern rest glyphs. The
source's rounded ending spans use conventional numbered volta brackets.
The final "M. D. C." is expanded to "Menuetto da capo". No additional
performance rule about repeats on the return has been imposed.

Line breaks, page breaks, instrument labels, and measure numbers are
editorial layout aids.

WORN GLYPHS AND EDITORIAL READINGS
Staff position and surviving ledger lines are the primary evidence;
parallel passages, voice leading, and harmony help resolve damaged heads.
Worn ink can leave a remnant above a note's actual center, so the highest
surviving ink alone is not treated as its pitch. Harmonic plausibility is
used to interpret unclear notation, not to regularize clear dissonances.

1. Menuetto m. 16, viola, beat 3: D4 is read as a quarter note after
   the half-note B-flat3. The pale, hollow-looking head is consistent
   with wear; the 3/4 meter and matching cello rhythm support a quarter.
   The earlier description of a printed half-note error was too strong.
2. Menuetto m. 28, cello: the final two noteheads are degraded. The bar
   is read as three quarters, E-flat3, B-flat2, E-flat2, matching the
   rhythm and contour of the viola's closing bar.
3. High notes in Trio mm. 9 and 28: the viola's E-flat5 and cello's
   E-flat4 are retained. Ledger relationships, octave doubling, and
   their resolution to D in the next measure support these readings.
